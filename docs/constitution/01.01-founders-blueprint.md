## Preamble

BASE is more than its current application. Products, technologies, markets, and business models may change. The company's purpose, duties, and standards must remain understandable through those changes.

This Blueprint is the founder's statement of how BASE should be owned, governed, financed, led, and protected. It defines the principles that apply when commercial opportunity, investor pressure, employee interests, customer needs, and long-term trust point in different directions.

The Blueprint is an internal governance document. It does not replace articles of association, shareholder agreements, employment agreements, board rules, or applicable law. Where a binding legal document conflicts with this Blueprint, the legal document governs the immediate legal question and the conflict must be documented for founder and board review.

## 1. Constitutional hierarchy

BASE uses the following hierarchy:

1. Applicable law and binding corporate agreements.
2. The approved BASE Constitution and this Founder's Blueprint.
3. Board-approved company policies and annual strategy.
4. Product Requirements Documents and Business Playbooks.
5. Technical Design Documents and operating procedures.
6. Team plans, project decisions, and individual judgment.

Lower-level documents may add detail but must not silently weaken a higher-level principle.

## 2. Corporate purpose

BASE exists to build a durable company that helps athletes, coaches, and sporting organizations make better decisions and create better long-term development.

The company is not established to maximize screen time, data extraction, dependence, or short-term transaction volume. It creates value by earning trust, improving real-world work, and sustaining a business capable of serving customers over time.

No current product implementation is constitutionally protected. The problem BASE serves and the standard by which it serves it are more important than any particular app, feature, platform, model, or technology.

## 3. Vision and time horizon

BASE aims to become an enduring international sports-technology company and a trusted institutional partner for serious training.

The company plans across three horizons:

- **Now:** prove repeatable customer value and a viable economic engine.
- **Next:** build organizational capability, distribution, and defensible knowledge.
- **Long term:** become infrastructure that outlives individual founders, products, and technology cycles.

BASE should favor decisions that compound trust, capability, and strategic freedom over five to ten years. Long-term thinking must not be used to excuse weak execution or indefinite losses.

## 4. Stakeholder duty

BASE is accountable to several stakeholders: athletes, coaches, customers, employees, shareholders, partners, communities, and regulators.

When interests conflict, decisions follow this order:

1. Law, safety, human dignity, and fundamental rights.
2. Honesty toward customers and people affected by BASE decisions.
3. Long-term company viability and product trust.
4. Fair treatment of employees, partners, and shareholders.
5. Sustainable growth and financial return.
6. Short-term revenue, valuation, or engagement.

This order does not remove the duty to create returns. It defines the boundaries within which returns are legitimate.

## 5. Ownership philosophy

Ownership of BASE is stewardship, not entitlement.

Shareholders should contribute capital, capability, access, or long-term support consistent with the company's purpose. The cap table must remain understandable enough that incentives, control, and future financing options are visible.

BASE will:

- protect sufficient founder and board authority to preserve the mission during the validation stage;
- avoid unnecessary dilution and opaque side agreements;
- document vesting, leaver provisions, transfer restrictions, and information rights;
- evaluate investors by alignment, reputation, contribution, and behavior—not capital alone;
- model the consequences of every financing round before commitment;
- avoid giving one commercial partner control over the company's strategic freedom.

No person should retain permanent operational authority solely because they were early.

## 6. Founder obligations

Founders accept duties beyond their job titles. Each founder must:

- act in the company's interest rather than use the company as personal property;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest;
- protect confidential information and intellectual property;
- maintain reliable records of material commitments and decisions;
- challenge misconduct even when confrontation is costly;
- develop successors and reduce key-person dependency;
- distinguish company expenses from personal expenses;
- honor agreed roles, time commitments, and decision processes;
- accept that leadership authority may change as the company grows.

Founder status does not exempt anyone from performance, conduct, or governance standards.

## 7. Founder roles and accountability

Each founder role must have a written mandate defining outcomes, decision rights, budget authority, reporting duties, and review cadence.

At least annually, the Board evaluates whether founder roles still match the company's needs. A founder may remain an owner while no longer holding an executive position.

The CEO is accountable for company performance, leadership, resource allocation, and execution of board-approved strategy. The Board appoints, supports, evaluates, and, where necessary, replaces the CEO.

## 8. Governance bodies

### General meeting

Shareholders exercise the rights granted by law and binding agreements, including election of the Board and approval of reserved ownership matters.

### Board

The Board governs the company, appoints and supervises executive leadership, approves strategy and risk appetite, protects solvency, and represents the long-term interests of the company.

The Board must receive accurate, timely information and preserve minutes for material decisions. As BASE grows, the Board should add independent competence in finance, technology, sport, governance, and international scaling.

### Executive leadership

Executive leadership operates the company within approved strategy, policy, budget, and risk limits. It owns results, not merely activity.

### Advisory groups

Advisors provide expertise but hold no decision authority unless formally delegated. Advisory titles must not create ambiguity about accountability.

## 9. Reserved matters

The following require explicit founder and/or Board approval according to binding governance documents:

- changing corporate purpose, mission, or constitutional principles;
- issuing shares, options, convertibles, warrants, or similar rights;
- taking material debt or granting security;
- approving or materially changing the annual budget;
- entering a new country, regulated activity, or materially different business line;
- acquiring or selling a company, substantial asset, or material intellectual property;
- signing an exclusive partnership that limits strategic freedom;
- making material changes to privacy, safety, or data-commercialization policy;
- hiring or dismissing the CEO and other designated key executives;
- related-party transactions;
- litigation, settlement, or regulatory commitments above an agreed threshold;
- sale, merger, liquidation, or change of control.

Thresholds and voting rules must be specified in binding corporate documents, not left to custom.

## 10. Decision system

Decisions are classified by reversibility and consequence:

- **Type 1:** difficult to reverse, company-level, or capable of causing material legal, financial, human, or reputational harm. Requires written analysis and designated approval.
- **Type 2:** reversible within normal operating limits. Delegated owners should decide quickly and document the result proportionally.

A material decision record should state:

- the decision and owner;
- the problem and relevant evidence;
- alternatives considered;
- financial, legal, human, ethical, and strategic effects;
- conflicts of interest;
- success and stop conditions;
- review date.

Consensus is useful but not mandatory. Clarity of authority is mandatory.

## 11. Founder conflict and deadlock

Disagreement is expected. Avoiding it is not governance.

Founder conflicts follow this sequence:

1. Write the disputed decision, facts, assumptions, and authority.
2. Separate personal interests from company interests.
3. Seek relevant independent evidence or advice.
4. Attempt a time-bounded resolution between the founders.
5. Escalate to the Board or agreed independent mediator.
6. Apply the voting, deadlock, buy-sell, or transfer provisions in binding agreements.

No founder may use employees, customers, public channels, or operational disruption as leverage in an internal ownership conflict.

## 12. Integrity and conflicts of interest

BASE does not tolerate bribery, fraud, retaliation, discrimination, deliberate deception, undisclosed self-dealing, or manipulation of company records.

Directors, founders, and employees must disclose personal, family, financial, or external interests that could affect their judgment. Related-party transactions require independent review, fair terms, and documented approval.

Performance pressure never authorizes dishonest reporting or concealment of risk.

## 13. People philosophy

BASE hires for capability, judgment, integrity, learning speed, and collaboration. Prestige and familiarity are not substitutes for evidence of fit.

The company owes people:

- clear roles and expectations;
- lawful and fair employment conditions;
- direct feedback and meaningful performance review;
- psychological and physical safety;
- equal opportunity and freedom from harassment;
- appropriate tools, information, and decision access;
- respectful treatment during restructuring or dismissal.

Employees owe the company reliable execution, honest communication, confidentiality, responsible use of resources, and conduct consistent with the company's standards.

## 14. Leadership culture

Leaders at BASE must:

- say what is true, especially when the news is unfavorable;
- distinguish facts, estimates, assumptions, and aspirations;
- make ownership visible;
- raise risks before they become crises;
- decide at the lowest competent level;
- reward responsible dissent;
- correct systems, not only individuals;
- protect focus by stopping work;
- avoid hero cultures and preventable dependency on single people.

Speed is valuable only when it produces learning or results without creating unacceptable hidden risk.

## 15. Compensation and incentives

Compensation should be fair, explainable, affordable, and aligned with sustained company value.

BASE will:

- define salary bands or equivalent principles as the organization grows;
- separate performance assessment from personal favoritism;
- grant equity with written vesting, dilution, tax, and leaver terms;
- avoid incentives that reward harmful sales, unsafe shortcuts, or distorted reporting;
- review executive incentives for unintended consequences;
- honor earned compensation and communicate changes before they take effect.

Equity is ownership with risk and responsibility—not a substitute for paying people fairly where the company can do so.

## 16. Intellectual property and institutional memory

Work created for BASE must be owned or validly licensed by the company under written agreements.

Critical knowledge must not live only in a founder's inbox, personal account, private device, or memory. The company maintains controlled repositories for legal documents, financial records, source code, product decisions, customer commitments, policies, and incident history.

Open-source software, third-party data, trademarks, content, models, and patents must be used under understood rights and obligations.

## 17. Capital allocation

Capital is allocated to protect solvency, create validated customer value, build durable capability, meet legal obligations, and expand only where the company has earned the right.

Priority order:

1. Payroll, tax, contractual obligations, security, and regulatory duties.
2. Reliability and service commitments to existing customers.
3. Validation of the core economic engine.
4. Capabilities that remove demonstrated constraints.
5. Growth with measurable payback or strategic value.
6. Speculative initiatives with explicit loss limits.

Every material investment requires an owner, expected outcome, budget, review date, and stop condition.

## 18. Financial discipline

BASE maintains accurate accounts, approval limits, separation of duties proportionate to company size, and a rolling cash forecast.

Leadership and the Board must understand:

- cash balance, runway, burn, and committed liabilities;
- revenue quality and concentration;
- gross margin and variable service cost;
- tax and payroll obligations;
- budget variance;
- financing needs and downside scenarios.

Revenue is not treated as healthy when it depends on unsustainable support, hidden customization, unacceptable liability, or strategic dependence.

## 19. Financing principles

BASE may be financed through customer revenue, founder capital, grants, debt, or equity. The source must fit the company's stage, risk, repayment ability, and strategic needs.

Before accepting external capital, BASE must document:

- what constraint the capital removes;
- why the amount is appropriate;
- the runway and milestones created;
- dilution and control consequences;
- downside and follow-on financing risk;
- investor rights and strategic restrictions;
- the alternative of slower growth.

Valuation is not success. Capital raised is a liability to execute responsibly.

## 20. Business-model standards

BASE earns revenue by delivering clear, lawful, and durable customer value.

The company will not build a business model primarily dependent on deceptive design, hidden renewal, artificial addiction, sale of sensitive personal data, fear-based marketing, or customers being unable to leave.

Pricing, contract terms, renewal, cancellation, and material limitations must be understandable before purchase. Commercial experiments must have evidence, boundaries, and a documented decision rule.

## 21. Customers and market conduct

BASE will sell what it can responsibly deliver. Sales forecasts, product claims, references, and case studies must distinguish verified outcomes from hypotheses.

Customer commitments that create material legal, technical, or operational obligations require review by the relevant owners before signature. No salesperson, founder, or executive may commit hidden roadmap work merely to close a deal.

Complaints, cancellations, incidents, and lost deals are treated as governance information—not only commercial inconvenience.

## 22. Partnerships and dependency

Partnerships must create measurable value, preserve lawful data use, protect the brand, and include a practical exit path.

Before a material partnership, BASE evaluates:

- strategic fit and customer benefit;
- reputation and counterparty conduct;
- exclusivity and lock-in;
- economics and operational cost;
- intellectual-property and data rights;
- security and compliance;
- concentration and continuity risk;
- termination and transition.

No partner should become able to dictate BASE's purpose through technical, commercial, or financial dependency.

## 23. Expansion, acquisitions, and new ventures

BASE enters a new market, country, sport, or business line only when leadership can explain why the company has an advantage, what capabilities are required, how the expansion will be governed, and what must remain true in the core business.

Acquisitions require strategic logic beyond revenue addition. Due diligence must cover finance, tax, legal obligations, people, technology, data rights, security, culture, customer concentration, and integration cost.

Every material expansion requires success criteria and an exit or stop plan.

## 24. Risk management

BASE maintains a company risk register appropriate to its stage. Risks are assessed by likelihood, impact, velocity, detectability, owner, mitigation, and residual exposure.

At minimum, the company monitors:

- solvency and financing;
- legal and regulatory compliance;
- privacy, cybersecurity, and data loss;
- customer and supplier concentration;
- product safety and professional liability;
- key-person dependency;
- intellectual-property ownership;
- employment and workplace conduct;
- fraud and financial control;
- reputation and public claims;
- operational continuity.

Material risks must be reported upward even when they threaten a target, launch, financing, or individual's standing.

## 25. Privacy, data, and AI as corporate duties

Detailed product controls belong in the PRD and TDD. At company level, BASE commits to lawful purpose, data minimization, appropriate security, access control, retention limits, deletion rights, traceability, and meaningful human accountability.

BASE will not treat customer or athlete data as an unrestricted corporate asset. Rights to use data must be documented and understandable.

AI does not receive independent corporate authority. The company remains accountable for systems it deploys, claims it makes, and decisions it automates.

## 26. Compliance and regulated boundaries

BASE identifies applicable obligations before entering regulated activities or markets. Management must obtain qualified legal, tax, accounting, security, and domain advice when internal competence is insufficient.

The company must not describe a product as unregulated merely because regulation is inconvenient or uncertain. Where classification is unclear, the uncertainty, advice, decision, and review trigger must be recorded.

## 27. Crisis and incident governance

BASE maintains proportionate response plans for security incidents, data loss, product-safety concerns, fraud, service failure, legal claims, financial distress, and public controversy.

During a material incident:

1. Protect people and prevent further harm.
2. Establish facts and preserve evidence.
3. Assign an incident leader and decision authority.
4. Meet notification and legal duties.
5. Communicate honestly without speculation.
6. Restore service or provide a safe alternative.
7. Document lessons and corrective actions.

The company does not conceal incidents to protect a launch, valuation, partnership, or individual.

## 28. Transparency and reporting

Shareholders, the Board, leaders, and employees receive information appropriate to their rights and responsibilities.

Company reporting must avoid vanity metrics and present material negative information with the same discipline as positive information. Forecasts should show assumptions and ranges where precision is not justified.

Confidentiality is used to protect legitimate interests, not to prevent accountability.

## 29. Succession and continuity

BASE must become capable of operating without any single founder, executive, employee, vendor, or customer.

The Board maintains succession and emergency coverage for critical roles. Access, signing authority, credentials, key relationships, and essential operating knowledge must be recoverable by the company.

Founder identity may shape BASE, but company survival must not depend on founder presence.

## 30. Founder departure, disability, or death

Binding agreements must address vesting, good- and bad-leaver treatment, transfer rights, valuation method, intellectual property, confidentiality, board rights, and continuity if a founder leaves, becomes unable to serve, or dies.

The company should respond with fairness and humanity while protecting continuity and avoiding governance paralysis.

## 31. Change of control and exit

BASE is not built solely to be sold. A sale, merger, or public listing may be appropriate when it advances stakeholder value without violating core duties.

Before supporting a change of control, decision makers evaluate:

- treatment of customers and sensitive data;
- continuity of commitments;
- employee consequences;
- mission and brand integrity;
- buyer reputation and strategic intent;
- shareholder return and distribution;
- legal, tax, and execution risk;
- credible alternatives.

No exit outcome justifies deception, hidden liabilities, or transferring data beyond lawful and communicated rights.

## 32. Dissolution

If BASE cannot continue responsibly, leadership and the Board must protect employees, customers, creditors, data, intellectual property, and legal obligations through an orderly process.

Dissolution planning must address access shutdown, data export and deletion, customer communication, contractual transition, statutory records, creditor priority, and remaining assets.

## 33. Amendment process

This Blueprint is a living constitution but not a casual document.

An amendment requires:

1. A written proposal stating the problem and intended consequence.
2. Identification of affected stakeholders and binding documents.
3. Review by the founder and Board, plus qualified advisors where relevant.
4. Approval under the reserved-matter rules.
5. Version update, change log, and communication to affected owners.

Temporary commercial pressure is not sufficient reason to weaken a principle without explicitly acknowledging the trade-off.

## 34. Annual constitutional review

At least annually, the founder and Board review:

- whether purpose and stakeholder duty remain clear;
- ownership, dilution, and control;
- founder and executive role fit;
- financial resilience and capital allocation;
- people, conduct, and incentive risks;
- customer and partner concentration;
- material legal, privacy, security, and safety exposure;
- succession and continuity;
- conflicts between this Blueprint and operating practice.

The review produces documented actions, owners, and deadlines.

## 35. Founder covenant

We commit to build BASE as stewards of a company that must be worthy of trust.

We will not confuse ownership with immunity, capital with success, growth with health, or technology with authority.

We will tell the truth about performance and risk. We will protect people and the company when doing so is inconvenient. We will create institutions capable of challenging us and succeeding after us.

We will make decisions that we can explain to customers, employees, shareholders, regulators, and our future successors without hiding the real trade-offs.

## 36. Acceptance criteria

This Blueprint is ready for approval when:

- it governs the company independently of the current BASE application;
- ownership, Board, management, and founder authority are distinguishable;
- reserved matters and escalation paths can be translated into binding agreements;
- capital, hiring, conduct, risk, succession, and exit principles are explicit;
- PRD, TDD, and Business Playbook can inherit from it without duplicating it;
- qualified legal and financial reviewers have identified which provisions require formal implementation.
