## 1. Purpose

This Blueprint defines the company BASE intends to become and the boundaries within which it will be built. It is the highest-level founder agreement beneath the approved BASE Constitution.

The Blueprint governs intent. The PRD governs what the product must do. The TDD governs how the system is built. The Business Playbook governs how BASE reaches, serves, and retains customers.

## 2. Founding thesis

Strength athletes and coaches operate across spreadsheets, messaging threads, generic fitness apps, memory, and intuition. Training plans are often static while the athlete is dynamic. Valuable context is lost between planning, execution, feedback, and adjustment.

BASE is founded on five beliefs:

1. High-quality training decisions should be easier to make and easier to explain.
2. Software should strengthen the coach-athlete relationship, never displace it.
3. Daily readiness is valuable only when it changes a decision responsibly.
4. Logged training matters when it improves the next action—not when it merely creates charts.
5. Trust is a product capability that must be designed, measured, and protected.

BASE begins with Olympic weightlifting because the domain is technically demanding, measurable, coach-led, underserved by general fitness software, and suitable for disciplined product learning. Expansion into adjacent strength sports must preserve domain quality.

## 3. Mission

BASE helps strength athletes and coaches turn plans, readiness, performance, and feedback into better daily training decisions.

## 4. Vision

BASE becomes the trusted operating system for serious strength training: the place where athletes train, coaches lead, clubs coordinate, and federations learn—without losing human judgment.

## 5. The promise

For the athlete:

> I know what to do today, why it is appropriate, and how to record it without leaving the training experience.

For the coach:

> I can see what matters, understand why the system recommends a change, and remain responsible for the training decision.

For the club:

> We can deliver a consistent coaching standard without turning athletes into rows in a spreadsheet.

BASE earns the right to expand only when these promises are repeatedly true.

## 6. Stakeholder hierarchy

When stakeholder interests conflict, BASE uses this order:

1. Athlete safety, dignity, and informed control.
2. Long-term training quality and coach accountability.
3. Product trust and data integrity.
4. Sustainable customer value.
5. Company growth and investor return.
6. Short-term engagement, conversion, or revenue.

Commercial performance is mandatory, but it must not be achieved by undermining the product's reason to exist.

## 7. Non-negotiable principles

### The coach remains a first-class authority

BASE may recommend, explain, warn, and automate reversible work. It must not obscure who made a consequential training decision. Athletes must be able to see whether a session came from a coach, a rule, an AI-assisted proposal, or their own edit.

### Recommendations must be explainable

Every material adjustment must show what changed, the original plan, the reason, the evidence used, who or what made the change, and how to undo or override it.

### Safety beats false certainty

BASE provides training support, not diagnosis or medical treatment. Pain, acute injury signals, and uncertain health conditions must route the user away from automated load prescriptions and toward an appropriate human decision.

### The smallest useful action wins

BASE removes unnecessary decisions, inputs, screens, and metrics. Data is shown only when it clarifies a decision, supports learning, or establishes accountability.

### Data exists inside a relationship of trust

BASE collects only data needed to deliver, validate, secure, or improve the service. Sensitive data requires a purpose, retention rule, access model, and deletion path.

### Evidence outranks hierarchy

Founder intuition may begin an experiment; it cannot declare the experiment successful. Claims and assumptions must be labeled as validated, provisional, or unknown.

### Reversibility is the default

Early product, pricing, architecture, and go-to-market decisions should remain reversible. Irreversible decisions require stronger evidence and explicit review.

## 8. What BASE is

BASE is a focused training workflow, a coach-athlete collaboration layer, a structured record of training and context, and a transparent decision-support system.

BASE is not a replacement for qualified coaching, a medical service, a social feed, a generic content library, an opaque AI coach, or an enterprise platform built before athlete-level value is proven.

## 9. Product doctrine

The primary product loop is:

1. Plan the training.
2. Assess relevant context.
3. Recommend a responsible action.
4. Let the athlete or coach decide.
5. Execute and log the session.
6. Capture outcome and feedback.
7. Improve the next plan.

Every major feature must strengthen at least one step without materially weakening another. Features are prioritized by problem frequency, consequence severity, user evidence, strategic fit, ability to validate, delivery cost, and trust risk.

## 10. AI doctrine

AI is a capability inside BASE, not the product's authority or identity.

AI may summarize history, detect patterns, draft programs for review, explain recommendations, reduce coach administration, and personalize education.

AI must not present medical conclusions, conceal uncertainty, make irreversible consequential changes without review, fabricate evidence, override coach controls, or optimize engagement at the expense of training quality.

Training-relevant AI outputs must be traceable to their inputs, model or rule version, and resulting user action.

## 11. Sports-science doctrine

Each readiness rule, progression rule, deload trigger, or programming recommendation must document its intended population, inputs, assumptions, expected benefit, limitations, safety boundaries, evidence quality, validation method, and review owner.

BASE must distinguish scientific evidence, internal heuristics, and personalized coach judgment.

## 12. Brand doctrine

BASE should feel calm, capable, direct, and serious: confident without arrogance, precise without coldness, ambitious without hype, and technically deep without unnecessary complexity.

BASE must never use shame, fear, injury anxiety, fake urgency, or unsupported performance promises to drive behavior or sales.

## 13. Company culture

BASE expects people to write down consequential decisions, raise risks early, distinguish fact from inference, test the smallest useful version, own outcomes across boundaries, disagree directly and respectfully, and protect user trust when nobody is watching.

Talent without reliability is not enough. Speed without learning is not progress.

## 14. Decision rights

Founder approval is required for mission, vision, stakeholder hierarchy, new sports or customer segments, material safety or privacy changes, strategic pricing changes, fundraising, major capital commitments, and constitutional changes.

The product owner controls reversible scope and sequencing within the approved PRD. The technical owner controls implementation within the TDD and security constraints. A qualified domain owner reviews rules that materially affect training recommendations, pain handling, or sports-science claims.

## 15. Decision test

Before a consequential decision, ask:

1. Which user problem does this solve?
2. What evidence shows the problem matters?
3. Does it strengthen the primary training loop?
4. Is it safe, explainable, and reversible?
5. Does it preserve coach and athlete agency?
6. What must be true for it to succeed?
7. How and when will we know?
8. What will we stop doing to make room?

## 16. Business doctrine

BASE will charge for durable value, not access to anxiety. Growth should come from better training workflows, retained athletes and coaches, trusted recommendations, time saved, club-level standardization, and referrals earned through results and usability.

Pricing must align with value, remain simple enough to explain in one minute, and remain testable before complexity hardens.

## 17. Moat thesis

Defensibility may emerge from the combination of a trusted coach-athlete workflow, structured longitudinal training data, domain-specific recommendation logic, explainable decision history, a strong exercise ontology, institutional relationships, workflow depth, and brand credibility.

No moat claim is valid until supported by retention, switching behavior, proprietary learning, or distribution advantage.

## 18. Capital doctrine

Capital removes validated constraints; it does not hide lack of product-market fit. BASE keeps fixed costs low until retention is demonstrated, funds experiments with explicit learning goals, avoids premature platform complexity, and treats runway as time available to learn.

## 19. Company scorecard

BASE maintains a compact scorecard covering weekly active athletes, active coaches, sessions started and completed, readiness use, recommendation acceptance and override, week-4 and week-12 retention, coach time saved, trust, paid conversion, revenue retention, and safety/privacy/reliability incidents.

Each metric requires a definition, owner, source, and decision consequence.

## 20. Strategic gates

1. **Workflow value** — Athletes can understand, complete, and log the core session flow.
2. **Trust** — Readiness and recommendations are understood and responsibly overridden.
3. **Coach value** — Coaches gain clarity or save time without losing control.
4. **Retention** — Users return across a meaningful training period.
5. **Monetization** — Customers pay at a sustainable support cost.
6. **Scale** — Sports, clubs, integrations, and markets expand only after prior gates hold.

## 21. Anti-patterns

BASE must reconsider when roadmap decisions follow competitor checklists, recommendation logic cannot be explained, users enter the same data twice, notifications compensate for weak value, dashboards lack decisions, AI lacks evaluation or fallback, sensitive data is collected “for later,” or enterprise promises fork the product prematurely.

## 22. Acceptance criteria

The Blueprint is ready for approval when it resolves plausible stakeholder conflicts, gives the founder a usable decision test, makes safety and data boundaries explicit, prevents premature scaling, aligns the PRD/TDD/Playbook, and contains no unsupported performance or medical claims.
