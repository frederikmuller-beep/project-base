# 01.00 — Document Control

## Purpose

This chapter defines how the BASE Constitution is owned, reviewed, approved, changed, and used. Its purpose is to preserve a single authoritative record of the principles that govern BASE as the product, company, and team evolve.

The Constitution is a decision system—not a collection of aspirations. Every approved chapter must be specific enough to guide product, design, coaching, sports science, AI, engineering, and commercial decisions without relying on undocumented context.

## Authority

The version merged into the repository's default branch is the canonical BASE Constitution.

If another source—such as a slide deck, design file, product specification, ticket, meeting note, AI output, or verbal instruction—conflicts with an approved constitutional rule, the Constitution takes precedence until the conflict is resolved through the change process defined below.

The Constitution does not replace operational specifications. It defines the principles, boundaries, and decision rules those specifications must follow.

## Status lifecycle

Each chapter moves through the following states:

1. **Draft** — Initial proposal under active development.
2. **In review** — Ready for structured founder and stakeholder review.
3. **Approved** — Explicitly accepted by the required approver.
4. **Effective** — Approved and merged into the default branch.
5. **Superseded** — Replaced by a later approved version.
6. **Retired** — No longer applicable, with the reason preserved in history.

Approval must be explicit. Silence, lack of comments, or the passage of time does not constitute approval.

## Approval and locking rule

A chapter is locked when it has been explicitly approved and merged.

A locked chapter may be changed only when at least one of the following conditions applies:

- BASE's product or company strategy has materially changed.
- New evidence shows that the existing rule is incomplete, unsafe, or incorrect.
- A legal, regulatory, security, privacy, or platform requirement makes a change necessary.
- The founders agree that the change improves the long-term integrity of BASE.

Editorial corrections that do not alter meaning may use an abbreviated review. Any change to intent, scope, authority, or decision rules requires a new review and approval.

## Change process

Every substantive change must:

1. Be made on a dedicated branch.
2. Be submitted through a pull request.
3. Explain what changed and why.
4. Identify affected chapters, product areas, and decisions.
5. Preserve unresolved disagreements in the review history.
6. Receive explicit approval before merge.
7. Update the version and change log.

No approved constitutional rule may be changed directly on the default branch.

## Versioning

Chapters use semantic document versions:

- **Major** — Changes the chapter's governing intent or reverses an approved principle.
- **Minor** — Adds a meaningful rule, section, or decision criterion without reversing the chapter.
- **Patch** — Clarifies wording or corrects errors without changing meaning.

File numbers express structure, not version. For example, `01.00` identifies the chapter's position; the version is recorded inside the chapter and in Git history.

## Chapter requirements

Unless a chapter explicitly documents why a section is not applicable, constitutional chapters should contain:

- Purpose
- Principle
- Rationale
- Consequences
- Practical examples
- Anti-patterns
- Decision rules
- Acceptance criteria
- References

Normative language must be interpreted consistently:

- **Must / must not** — Mandatory rule.
- **Should / should not** — Strong default; exceptions require a documented reason.
- **May** — Permitted but optional.
- **Principle** — Enduring guidance used when no specific rule applies.
- **Decision rule** — A test that produces or constrains an action.

## Reading guide

The Constitution should be read in three layers:

1. **Foundation** — Why BASE exists and the principles that must remain stable.
2. **Domain charters** — How product, brand, coaching, sports science, AI, and engineering apply the foundation.
3. **Operational consequences** — The acceptance criteria and decision rules used in day-to-day work.

Readers should begin with the Executive Summary, then consult the relevant domain charter before making a consequential decision. Related implementation documents may add detail but may not silently weaken an approved rule.

## Review responsibilities

The document owner is responsible for:

- maintaining structural consistency;
- identifying conflicts between chapters;
- ensuring claims and references are reviewable;
- keeping status, version, and history current;
- preventing unapproved text from being treated as policy.

Reviewers are responsible for challenging ambiguity, unsupported claims, conflicting incentives, impractical rules, and language that cannot be tested in real decisions.
